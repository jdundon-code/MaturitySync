[Message 6 - User]
What are reasons that certificates roll over with no customer or member interaction?

---

[Message 7 - Assistant]
Here are the common reasons that certificates (CDs/share certificates) roll over automatically without customer or member interaction:

1. **Default contractual terms** – Most certificate agreements state that if the member takes no action by the maturity date, the certificate automatically renews into a new term of the same or similar length.

2. **Grace period expiration** – Institutions typically offer a short grace period (e.g., 7–10 days) after maturity for the member to act. If no action is taken during this window, the rollover triggers automatically.

3. **Unreachable member** – The institution may attempt to notify the member of the upcoming maturity, but if contact information is outdated (wrong address, old email, disconnected phone), the member never receives the notice and the certificate rolls over by default.

4. **Member inattention/unawareness** – Members may simply forget, miss the notification, or not realize the maturity date is approaching—especially for longer-term certificates opened years ago.

5. **Deceased or incapacitated member** – If the account holder has passed away or become incapacitated and no beneficiary or POA has taken action, the certificate will roll per its terms.

6. **Assumed satisfaction** – Some members are aware of the maturity but intentionally take no action because they're satisfied with the current rate environment or simply want to keep the funds locked away.

7. **Notification failures** – System issues, mail delivery failures, spam filters catching emails, or the institution failing to send timely maturity notices can all result in the member not being aware of the upcoming event.

8. **Institutional policy/regulatory compliance** – Reg DD (Truth in Savings) requires disclosure of renewal policies upfront, but the default behavior written into the agreement is almost always auto-renewal, making rollover the path of least resistance.

9. **Joint account coordination gaps** – On joint accounts, each owner may assume the other is handling it, resulting in no one taking action.

10. **Small balance accounts** – Members may deprioritize low-balance certificates, letting them roll over because the effort to close or redirect feels disproportionate to the amount.

From a product/systems perspective, this is important because auto-rollovers can lock members into unfavorable rates and create service friction later. Many FIs are now building proactive maturity notification workflows and digital self-service options to reduce unintentional rollovers.

Is this an opportunity to increase deposits at a financial institution?

---

[Message 9 - Assistant]
Yes, absolutely. Certificate maturity events are one of the highest-leverage moments for deposit growth at a financial institution. Here's why:

**The Opportunity**

At maturity, a member is effectively making a decision—whether they realize it or not—about where their money goes next. If the FI is proactive rather than passive, they can influence that decision in their favor.

**How it increases deposits:**

1. **Upsell to higher-balance certificates** – At maturity, prompt the member to add funds to their renewal. "Your $10,000 certificate is maturing—did you know our $25,000+ tier earns an additional 0.25% APY?" Even modest increases across the portfolio compound into meaningful deposit growth.

2. **Cross-sell into other deposit products** – Use the maturity event as a trigger to recommend money market accounts, high-yield savings, or certificate laddering strategies that keep funds at the institution rather than leaving in a low-rate rollover.

3. **Prevent attrition to competitors** – The biggest risk at maturity isn't just a rollover at an unfavorable rate—it's the member withdrawing and moving funds to a competitor offering a better rate. Proactive engagement (personalized rate offers, loyalty bonuses) keeps deposits in-house.

4. **Re-engage dormant relationships** – Many certificate holders are "set it and forget it" members with minimal engagement. The maturity event is a natural reason to reconnect, deepen the relationship, and uncover other deposit opportunities (e.g., "We noticed you don't have an emergency savings account with us...").

5. **Capture external funds** – When engaging a member about their maturing certificate, it's a natural moment to ask if they have funds at other institutions they'd like to consolidate—especially if you're offering a competitive renewal rate or a "new money" bonus.

6. **Certificate laddering programs** – Instead of a single rollover, guide members into a laddering strategy (e.g., split into 12-, 24-, and 36-month terms). This often results in the member committing additional funds to fill out the ladder.

7. **Retention pricing flexibility** – Offering a small rate bump or loyalty premium at renewal is far cheaper than acquiring a new depositor. The math favors keeping existing certificate holders engaged and growing.

**The Cost of Inaction**

When certificates silently roll over:
- Members may later discover an unfavorable rate and feel frustrated, leading to withdrawal
- The FI misses a warm, natural touchpoint to grow the relationship
- Competitors with better digital maturity experiences capture the member's attention first
- Deposit growth becomes purely acquisition-driven (expensive) rather than expansion-driven (efficient)

**Bottom line:** Every maturing certificate is a deposit growth event disguised as an operational process. FIs that treat maturity as a marketing/engagement moment—rather than just a back-office transaction—consistently outperform on deposit retention and growth.

**Problem Statement**

FIs that treat certificate maturity as a back-office transaction are being outperformed by FIs that treat it as an engagement opportunity.

**Why this matters:**

- **For the FI:** Silent rollovers represent hidden deposit attrition risk. Every maturity that passes without engagement is a missed chance to grow balances, deepen relationships, and defend against rate-shopping competitors. The FIs winning the deposit game are the ones converting operational events into relationship moments.

- **For the member:** A certificate maturing without their awareness or input often leads to a suboptimal outcome—locked into a rate they didn't choose, missing better options they weren't shown, or feeling like the institution doesn't value their business enough to reach out.

**The gap:**

Most core banking systems treat maturity as a batch process: certificate matures → auto-renew at current rate → done. There's no orchestration layer that turns that event into:
- A personalized notification sequence (pre-maturity, at maturity, grace period)
- A decision moment with clear options (renew, add funds, ladder, redirect to other products)
- An engagement touchpoint that strengthens the member relationship
- A data capture event that informs the FI's deposit strategy

**Who's winning and why:**

FIs that outperform on deposit growth around certificate maturity are doing three things differently:
1. **Proactive outreach** – Contacting members 30/15/7 days before maturity with personalized options
2. **Digital self-service** – Letting members choose their renewal path (amount, term, rate) without calling or visiting a branch
3. **Intelligent recommendations** – Using member data (balances, behavior, goals) to suggest the right next product, not just a blind rollover

**The opportunity for a product:**

Build the orchestration and engagement layer that sits between the core system's maturity event and the member experience—turning a silent back-office process into a guided, personalized deposit growth moment.

# CertificateIQ — Certificate Maturity Engagement Platform

## Product Vision

A digital-first engagement layer that transforms certificate maturity from a silent back-office event into a personalized, guided decision moment — increasing deposit retention, growing balances, and deepening member relationships.

## Who It's For

**Primary buyer:** Digital banking leaders, deposit strategists, and marketing teams at banks and credit unions ($500M–$50B+ in assets) who want to:
- Retain more deposits at maturity
- Grow certificate balances through add-on and upsell flows
- Reduce rate-shopping attrition
- Deepen single-product certificate relationships into multi-product households

**End user:** The member/customer approaching, at, or just past a certificate maturity date.

---

## Core Capabilities

### 1. Maturity Event Engine
- Ingests maturity dates from the core banking system (batch file, API, or real-time event)
- Builds a timeline for each certificate: 60/30/15/7/3/1 days pre-maturity, maturity day, grace period countdown
- Triggers engagement workflows based on configurable rules

### 2. Omnichannel Notification Orchestration
- **Push notifications** (mobile banking app)
- **In-app messages/interstitials** (when member opens digital banking)
- **Email sequences** (pre-maturity education → maturity decision → grace period urgency)
- **SMS** (opt-in, for high-balance or at-risk members)
- Configurable cadence, channel priority, and suppression rules per FI

### 3. Digital Self-Service Decision Hub
A member-facing experience (embedded in digital banking or standalone) that presents:
- **Current certificate details** — term, rate, balance, maturity date
- **Renewal options** — same term at current rate, alternative terms, rate comparison
- **Add funds flow** — "Add $5,000 to unlock the next rate tier"
- **Ladder builder** — visual tool to split maturing balance across multiple terms
- **Alternative product recommendations** — money market, high-yield savings, IRA certificates
- **Rate lock/hold** — reserve a rate for X days while deciding (if FI supports)
- One-tap renewal or redirect — no branch visit, no phone call

### 4. Intelligent Recommendations Engine
Uses member data to personalize the decision experience:
- **Balance tier proximity** — "You're $2,000 away from our premium rate"
- **Relationship depth scoring** — deeper relationships get loyalty rate offers
- **Rate environment context** — "Rates have moved since you opened this certificate"
- **Behavioral signals** — has the member been browsing rates? Logging in more frequently? Checking competitor sites?
- **Life stage indicators** — upcoming large expenses, account age, other product holdings

### 5. FI Configuration & Control Panel
- Set notification timing, channel mix, and messaging templates
- Configure which products/terms to recommend at maturity
- Define loyalty/retention rate premiums and eligibility rules
- A/B test messaging, timing, and offer strategies
- Set risk thresholds (high-balance, single-product, first-time maturity = higher priority)

### 6. Analytics & Reporting Dashboard
- **Retention rate by cohort** — term, balance tier, channel, timing
- **Attrition risk scoring** — which certificates are most likely to leave?
- **Revenue impact** — dollars retained vs. projected runoff
- **Engagement metrics** — notification open/click rates, self-service completion
- **Add-on success** — incremental deposits captured at renewal
- **Benchmark comparisons** — FI performance vs. industry (Curinos-style)

[Message 17 - Assistant]
Great question — and you're right to challenge that. Let me clarify the architecture reality:

## You're Correct: The Digital Banking Platform Already Has Maturity Dates

In most modern digital banking deployments, the digital banking platform (DBP) already pulls account-level data from the core — including certificate maturity dates, terms, rates, and balances. That data is already surfaced to the member on their account detail screens.

So you **don't** need a separate core integration to *get* maturity dates. The digital banking platform is already the system of record for presenting that data to the member.

## What You Actually Need

The value isn't in *reading* the maturity date — it's in **acting on it intelligently**. What's missing in most digital banking platforms today is:

1. **Event-driven orchestration** — The DBP knows the maturity date, but it doesn't *do* anything with it proactively. It's static data sitting on a screen. The gap is: who's watching that date and triggering engagement workflows at the right moments?

2. **Decision experience** — The DBP shows "Maturity Date: 09/15/2026" on the account detail page. It does NOT typically offer a guided renewal flow, product alternatives, add-fund prompts, or ladder-building tools as that date approaches.

3. **Notification logic** — Most DBPs don't have built-in maturity countdown notification sequences. They might send a single alert, but not an orchestrated multi-touch campaign.

4. **Recommendation intelligence** — The DBP displays what the member *has*. It doesn't calculate what the member *should do* based on their relationship, rate environment, and behavior.

## Revised Architecture Framing

So the right framing isn't "integrate with the core to get maturity data" — it's:

**Build an engagement layer ON TOP of the digital banking platform that:**
- Consumes the maturity data the DBP already has
- Adds orchestration logic (when to engage, through which channel, with what message)
- Provides the decision UX (the self-service renewal/redirect/add-funds experience)
- Delivers the intelligence layer (recommendations, risk scoring, personalization)

This positions the product as a **digital banking platform enhancement or plugin** rather than a core-to-product integration. Much lighter lift for implementation, faster time-to-value for the FI.

## Updated Integration Model

| Layer | What Exists | What's Added |
|-------|-------------|--------------|
| Core Banking | Maturity dates, rates, terms, balances | (unchanged) |
| Digital Banking Platform | Surfaces account data to member | Engagement SDK/widget embedded here |
| **CertificateIQ (new)** | — | Orchestration engine, decision UX, recommendations, analytics |

The product plugs into the DBP's existing data layer and extends it with engagement logic and member-facing decision experiences.

Thank you for the pushback — this is a meaningfully better architecture story. It makes the product easier to sell ("no core integration required, works with your existing digital banking data") and faster to deploy.
